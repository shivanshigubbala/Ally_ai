Headache Classification — Clinical Reference

OVERVIEW
Headache is one of the most common neurological complaints. The International Headache Society (IHS) classifies headaches in the International Classification of Headache Disorders (ICHD-3) into primary headaches (no underlying cause) and secondary headaches (due to underlying condition). Accurate classification guides appropriate treatment.

PRIMARY HEADACHES

1. TENSION-TYPE HEADACHE (TTH) — Most Common Headache
Subtypes: Episodic (<15 days/month) and Chronic (≥15 days/month for >3 months)

Features:
- Bilateral, pressing/squeezing quality ("tight band around head")
- Mild to moderate intensity
- Not aggravated by physical activity
- No nausea/vomiting (or at most one)
- May have phonophobia OR photophobia but not both

Treatment (episodic):
- Simple analgesics: paracetamol 1g, ibuprofen 400mg, aspirin 900mg
- Combination analgesics with caffeine
- Avoid opioids and codeine (medication overuse risk)

Prevention (chronic):
- Amitriptyline 25-75mg at night (first-line)
- Mirtazapine, venlafaxine
- Non-pharmacological: stress management, physiotherapy, CBT, biofeedback

2. MIGRAINE — See Dedicated Migraine Document

3. TRIGEMINAL AUTONOMIC CEPHALALGIAS (TACs)

Cluster Headache:
- Predominantly male (3:1)
- Circadian pattern (attacks at same time daily, often nocturnal)
- Unilateral, periorbital/temporal, severe ("suicide headache")
- Duration: 15-180 minutes
- Frequency: 1-8 per day during cluster period (weeks to months)
- Ipsilateral autonomic features: lacrimation, conjunctival injection, rhinorrhea, ptosis, miosis, facial flushing
- Cluster periods separated by remission months/years

Acute treatment:
- 100% oxygen via non-rebreather mask 12-15L/min for 15 min (most effective)
- Subcutaneous sumatriptan 6mg (fastest acting)
- Intranasal zolmitriptan, sumatriptan nasal spray

Prevention (during cluster period):
- Verapamil 240-480mg (drug of choice for prevention)
- Lithium carbonate (chronic cluster)
- Prednisolone (short course to break cluster)
- Greater occipital nerve blocks
- CGRP monoclonal antibodies (galcanezumab)

Paroxysmal Hemicrania:
- Similar to cluster but shorter (2-30 min), more frequent (>5/day)
- Female predominance
- ABSOLUTE RESPONSE to indomethacin 75-225mg/day (diagnostic)

Hemicrania Continua:
- Continuous unilateral headache with exacerbations + autonomic features
- ABSOLUTE RESPONSE to indomethacin (diagnostic)

SUNCT/SUNA (Short-lasting Unilateral Neuralgiform headache attacks):
- Very brief (5-240 sec), many attacks per day
- Stabbing or electric shock
- Trigeminal neuralgia territory
- Treatment: lamotrigine

4. MEDICATION OVERUSE HEADACHE (MOH)
- Also called "rebound headache"
- Most important complication of primary headache treatment
- Develops after overuse of:
  - Triptans, ergotamines: >10 days/month
  - Simple analgesics (paracetamol, NSAIDs, aspirin): >15 days/month
  - Opioids, combination analgesics: >10 days/month
- Paradoxically increases headache frequency
- Treatment: withdrawal of overused medication
  - Often worsens initially (2-10 days withdrawal headache)
  - Prednisolone bridge therapy: 60mg → taper over 6 days
  - Inpatient withdrawal for severe cases
  - Preventive medication started during withdrawal

SECONDARY HEADACHES — DANGEROUS CAUSES

RED FLAGS (SNOOP4):
- Systemic symptoms (fever, weight loss, rash)
- Neurological deficits (focal weakness, vision loss, confusion)
- Onset sudden (thunderclap headache)
- Older age (>50 first headache)
- Previous headache history change (new pattern)
- Positional component (worse lying flat or upright)
- Papilledema, pregnancy

1. SUBARACHNOID HEMORRHAGE (SAH)
- Thunderclap headache: sudden severe headache, maximal intensity at onset ("worst headache of life")
- May follow exertion, sexual activity, Valsalva
- Neck stiffness, photophobia, vomiting
- LOC in severe cases
- Investigation: non-contrast CT head (90% sensitive in 6 hours) → LP if CT negative (xanthochromia after 12h)
- Treatment: neurosurgical emergency, coil/clip aneurysm

2. MENINGITIS
- Gradual severe headache with fever, neck stiffness, photophobia
- Classic triad (fever + neck stiffness + altered consciousness) only in 44%
- Kernig's sign, Brudzinski's sign
- Bacterial meningitis: medical emergency — IV antibiotics immediately (dexamethasone before first dose)
- LP: opening pressure, WBC, protein, glucose, culture, PCR

3. GIANT CELL ARTERITIS (TEMPORAL ARTERITIS)
- Age >50 (usually >70), female predominance
- Temporal headache, scalp tenderness (brush hair pain), jaw claudication
- Visual disturbances → irreversible blindness if untreated
- Elevated ESR (>50), CRP
- Treatment: HIGH DOSE prednisolone IMMEDIATELY (do not wait for biopsy)
- Temporal artery biopsy to confirm

4. IDIOPATHIC INTRACRANIAL HYPERTENSION (IIH) / PSEUDOTUMOR CEREBRI
- Young overweight women, oral contraceptive pill use
- Raised intracranial pressure without mass lesion
- Daily headache, pulsatile tinnitus, visual obscurations
- Papilledema on fundoscopy
- LP: elevated opening pressure (>25 cmH2O), normal CSF
- MRI: empty sella, optic nerve sheath distension, flattening of posterior globes
- Treatment: weight loss (most effective), acetazolamide, serial LP, ventriculoperitoneal shunt for vision threatening

5. CERVICOGENIC HEADACHE
- Headache originating from cervical spine structures
- Unilateral, occipital/cervical origin, referred to forehead
- Restricted neck movement, tender cervical muscles
- Diagnostic: nerve blocks
- Treatment: physiotherapy, nerve blocks, surgery if necessary

6. POST-TRAUMATIC HEADACHE
- Develops within 7 days of head trauma
- Can persist >3 months (persistent post-traumatic headache)
- Often migraine-like or tension-type features
- Treat like primary headache type it resembles

ASSESSMENT APPROACH
History:
- Site, onset (sudden vs. gradual), character, severity, radiation
- Duration, frequency, pattern (episodic vs. constant)
- Associated symptoms (nausea, visual aura, autonomic features)
- Triggers, relieving factors
- Medication use
- Family history, previous headaches

Examination:
- Neurological examination (focal signs)
- Fundoscopy (papilledema)
- Blood pressure
- Neck stiffness
- Temporal artery tenderness
- Cervical spine examination
