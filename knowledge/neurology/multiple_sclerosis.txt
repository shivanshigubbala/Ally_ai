Multiple Sclerosis — Clinical Reference

DEFINITION
Multiple sclerosis (MS) is a chronic inflammatory demyelinating disease of the central nervous system (CNS) characterized by episodes of immune-mediated destruction of the myelin sheath and underlying axons in the brain and spinal cord. It is the leading non-traumatic cause of neurological disability in young adults.

EPIDEMIOLOGY
- Affects ~2.8 million people worldwide
- Most common diagnosis: 20-40 years, female predominance (3:1)
- Higher prevalence in temperate climates (latitude gradient)
- Higher risk: Northern European ancestry, family history, Epstein-Barr virus infection, vitamin D deficiency, smoking

PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
- Autoreactive T cells (CD4+) cross blood-brain barrier
- Target myelin antigens: MBP, PLP, MOG
- Demyelination → impaired nerve conduction → neurological symptoms
- Partial or complete remyelination → recovery
- Progressive axonal loss → permanent disability
- Plaques (lesions) in white matter: periventricular, juxtacortical, infratentorial, spinal cord

CLINICAL COURSES
1. Relapsing-Remitting MS (RRMS): 85% at onset
   - Episodes (relapses) of neurological dysfunction lasting >24h
   - Followed by partial or complete recovery
   - Most will convert to SPMS

2. Secondary Progressive MS (SPMS):
   - Gradual neurological decline after initial relapsing course
   - With or without superimposed relapses

3. Primary Progressive MS (PPMS): 15% at onset
   - Gradual neurological decline from onset without relapses
   - Older age onset, equal sex distribution

4. Clinically Isolated Syndrome (CIS):
   - First demyelinating episode not yet meeting MS criteria
   - MRI findings determine risk of conversion to MS

COMMON PRESENTATIONS
Optic neuritis (most common first symptom):
- Painful vision loss, color desaturation, afferent pupillary defect
- 50% recover completely

Spinal cord syndrome:
- Motor weakness, sensory level, bladder/bowel dysfunction
- Lhermitte's sign: electric shock sensation down spine on neck flexion

Brainstem/cerebellar syndromes:
- Internuclear ophthalmoplegia (INO): impaired horizontal gaze (MLF lesion)
- Nystagmus, diplopia
- Ataxia, tremor, dysarthria
- Vertigo, facial sensory loss

Other:
- Fatigue (most common symptom overall, ~80%)
- Cognitive impairment (information processing speed, memory)
- Depression, anxiety
- Uhthoff's phenomenon: worsening with heat
- Neuropathic pain

DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA (McDonald 2017)
Requires demonstration of dissemination in time (DIT) and space (DIS) in the CNS:
- DIS: ≥1 T2 lesion in ≥2 of 4 typical locations (periventricular, cortical/juxtacortical, infratentorial, spinal cord)
- DIT: simultaneous Gd-enhancing and non-enhancing lesions, or new T2/Gd+ lesion on follow-up MRI, or positive CSF (OCBs)

INVESTIGATIONS
MRI brain and spine:
- FLAIR: periventricular lesions ("Dawson fingers" perpendicular to ventricles)
- T2: white matter hyperintensities
- Gadolinium-enhancing: active inflammation (blood-brain barrier disruption)
- Black holes (T1 hypointense): axonal loss

CSF:
- Oligoclonal bands (OCBs): present in 90-95% of MS, absent in serum
- IgG index elevated
- Mildly elevated white cells in active disease

Visual evoked potentials (VEPs):
- Prolonged P100 latency in optic neuritis

Blood tests (to exclude mimics):
- ANA, ANCA, antiphospholipid antibodies (SLE, vasculitis)
- AQP4-IgG (Neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder, NMOSD)
- MOG-IgG (MOGAD)
- B12, folate, syphilis, HIV, Lyme

DISEASE-MODIFYING THERAPIES (DMTs)
Moderate efficacy (first-line):
- Interferon beta (IFN-beta 1a, 1b): injectable, reduce relapse rate ~30%
- Glatiramer acetate: injectable, similar efficacy to interferons
- Dimethyl fumarate (Tecfidera): oral, GI side effects, lymphopenia
- Teriflunomide (Aubagio): oral, teratogenic, monitor LFTs

High efficacy (second-line/highly active MS):
- Natalizumab (Tysabri): IV monthly, anti-VLA-4, risk of PML (JC virus)
- Alemtuzumab (Lemtrada): IV 2 courses, profound immune reconstitution, secondary autoimmunity
- Ocrelizumab (Ocrevus): IV biannual, anti-CD20 B-cell depletion, approved for RRMS and PPMS
- Ofatumumab (Kesimpta): SC monthly, anti-CD20
- Cladribine (Mavenclad): oral, 2 annual courses
- Siponimod (Mayzent): approved for active SPMS

ACUTE RELAPSE MANAGEMENT
- IV methylprednisolone 1g/day for 3-5 days
- Shortens duration but doesn't change long-term outcome
- Severe relapses: plasma exchange (PLEX)

SYMPTOM MANAGEMENT
Fatigue: amantadine, modafinil, energy conservation techniques
Spasticity: baclofen, tizanidine, physiotherapy, intrathecal baclofen
Bladder dysfunction: oxybutynin (overactive), intermittent self-catheterization (retention)
Neuropathic pain: gabapentin, pregabalin, amitriptyline, duloxetine
Depression: SSRIs, SNRIs, CBT
Tremor: clonazepam, propranolol, physiotherapy
Cognitive rehabilitation: structured programs, occupational therapy
Walking difficulty: fampridine (dalfampridine) — K+ channel blocker

PREGNANCY AND MS
- Relapses reduced during pregnancy (especially 3rd trimester)
- Increased relapse risk post-partum (3-6 months)
- Most DMTs contraindicated (natalizumab sometimes continued in pregnancy under specialist guidance)
- Breastfeeding generally compatible with interferons and glatiramer

MONITORING AND FOLLOW-UP
- Annual MRI to assess disease activity
- Regular neurological assessments (EDSS scoring)
- Safety monitoring for DMTs (CBC, LFTs, JC antibody index for natalizumab)
- Physiotherapy, occupational therapy involvement
- Multidisciplinary team approach
