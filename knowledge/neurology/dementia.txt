Dementia — Clinical Reference

DEFINITION
Dementia is a clinical syndrome of progressive cognitive decline severe enough to interfere with daily function. It encompasses multiple domains of cognition including memory, language, executive function, visuospatial ability, and personality/behavior. It is not a single disease but a group of symptoms caused by various brain disorders.

EPIDEMIOLOGY
- ~55 million people worldwide have dementia (2023)
- Alzheimer's disease accounts for 60-70% of cases
- Second most common: vascular dementia (~15-20%)
- Doubling every 20 years with aging population
- 70% of risk attributable to modifiable factors

MILD COGNITIVE IMPAIRMENT (MCI)
- Cognitive decline greater than expected for age but not meeting dementia criteria
- Daily function preserved
- Amnestic MCI: memory predominantly affected (high risk of Alzheimer's conversion ~10-15%/year)
- Non-amnestic MCI: other domains affected

ALZHEIMER'S DISEASE
Pathophysiology:
- Amyloid-beta plaques (extracellular) and neurofibrillary tau tangles (intracellular)
- Cholinergic neuron loss
- Hippocampal and entorhinal cortex affected first
- APOE4 allele: major genetic risk factor

Clinical features:
- Insidious onset, gradual progression
- Early: episodic memory impairment (forgetting recent events), word-finding difficulty
- Middle: spatial disorientation, behavioral changes, ADL impairment
- Late: severe memory loss, incontinence, dysphagia, bed-bound
- Behavioral symptoms: depression, anxiety, agitation, psychosis, wandering

Diagnostic workup:
- Clinical history (collateral history essential)
- Neuropsychological testing (MMSE, MoCA, ADAS-Cog)
- MRI brain: medial temporal lobe atrophy, hippocampal volume loss
- PET scan: amyloid PET (amyloid deposition), FDG-PET (hypometabolism pattern)
- CSF biomarkers: reduced Abeta42, elevated tau and phospho-tau
- Blood biomarkers (emerging): plasma p-tau217, p-tau181

VASCULAR DEMENTIA
- Caused by cerebrovascular disease: large artery strokes, small vessel disease (lacunar), white matter disease
- More stepwise progression, focal neurological signs
- Risk factors same as stroke
- MRI: white matter hyperintensities, lacunar infarcts
- Treatment: vascular risk factor management

LEWY BODY DEMENTIA (DLB)
Core features:
- Fluctuating cognition (variable attention and alertness)
- Recurrent visual hallucinations (vivid, detailed, typically animals or people)
- Parkinsonism (motor features)
- REM sleep behavior disorder

Supportive features:
- Severe neuroleptic sensitivity (avoid typical antipsychotics — risk of crisis)
- Low dopamine transporter uptake on DAT-SPECT

FRONTOTEMPORAL DEMENTIA (FTD)
- Younger onset (often 50-65 years)
- Behavioral variant FTD (bvFTD): disinhibition, apathy, compulsive behaviors, dietary changes, executive dysfunction
- Primary progressive aphasia (PPA): language variant
- TDP-43, FUS, tau pathology
- Genetic: C9orf72, GRN, MAPT mutations

PARKINSON'S DISEASE DEMENTIA (PDD)
- Dementia occurring >1 year after established PD
- Distinct from DLB (dementia concurrent with or before parkinsonism)
- Predominantly executive and visuospatial impairment

OTHER CAUSES
- Normal pressure hydrocephalus (NPH): triad of gait disorder, dementia, urinary incontinence; treatable with shunt
- Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD): rapid progression, myoclonus, periodic EEG, prion disease
- Autoimmune encephalitis: anti-NMDAR, LGI1, CASPR2 — treatable, younger patients
- Reversible causes: hypothyroidism, B12 deficiency, neurosyphilis, depression (pseudodementia)

ASSESSMENT TOOLS
- MMSE (Mini-Mental State Examination): 0-30, <24 suggests dementia
- MoCA (Montreal Cognitive Assessment): 0-30, <26 suggests MCI/dementia; more sensitive
- Clock Drawing Test, Trail Making Test (executive function)
- NPI (Neuropsychiatric Inventory) for behavioral symptoms

MANAGEMENT — PHARMACOLOGICAL
Cholinesterase inhibitors (Alzheimer's, DLB, PDD):
- Donepezil 5-10mg daily (best tolerated, once daily)
- Rivastigmine (oral or patch) — preferred in PDD/DLB
- Galantamine
- Side effects: nausea, diarrhea, bradycardia, nightmares

NMDA receptor antagonist:
- Memantine 10-20mg daily — for moderate-severe AD, mild benefit
- Can combine with donepezil in moderate-severe

Disease-modifying therapy (Alzheimer's):
- Lecanemab (Leqembi): anti-amyloid antibody, slows progression in early AD (2023 approval)
- Donanemab: similar mechanism
- Significant side effects: amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA)

Behavioral symptoms:
- Non-pharmacological first: behavioral interventions, environment modification
- Depression: SSRIs (sertraline, citalopram preferred)
- Psychosis/agitation: low-dose quetiapine, risperidone (avoid in DLB)
- Sleep: melatonin, low-dose trazodone

NON-PHARMACOLOGICAL
- Cognitive stimulation therapy
- Exercise programs
- Caregiver support and education (crucial)
- Advanced care planning (while capacity retained)
- Occupational therapy: home safety assessment
- Social services: day care, respite care

DRIVING AND LEGAL
- All patients with dementia should be assessed for driving fitness
- Early stage may still drive (with regular reassessment)
- Enduring power of attorney, advance directives — early planning essential
- Capacity assessment for medical decisions
