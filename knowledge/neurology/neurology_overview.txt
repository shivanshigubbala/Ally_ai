Neurology General Knowledge — Clinical Overview

NEUROLOGICAL EXAMINATION
Mental status:
- Consciousness: GCS (eye 1-4, verbal 1-5, motor 1-6)
- Orientation, attention, memory (immediate, short, long-term)
- Language: fluency, comprehension, repetition, naming
- Visuospatial function, executive function
- MMSE, MoCA for standardized assessment

Cranial nerves:
- CN I: smell (olfaction)
- CN II: visual acuity, visual fields, pupil reaction, fundoscopy
- CN III, IV, VI: extraocular movements, ptosis, pupil (PERRLA)
- CN V: facial sensation, mastication (jaw clench)
- CN VII: facial movements (forehead sparing in UMN lesion)
- CN VIII: hearing (Weber, Rinne), vestibular function
- CN IX, X: gag reflex, palate elevation, voice
- CN XI: sternomastoid, trapezius strength
- CN XII: tongue movements, fasciculations

Motor:
- Tone: spasticity (UMN), rigidity (basal ganglia), hypotonia (LMN, cerebellar)
- Power: MRC grading 0-5
- Reflexes: biceps (C5/6), brachioradialis (C6), triceps (C7), knee (L3/4), ankle (S1)
- Plantar responses (Babinski sign — UMN lesion if extensor)
- Clonus

Coordination:
- Finger-nose test, heel-shin test (cerebellar)
- Rapid alternating movements
- Gait: tandem, heel, toe walking
- Romberg test (eyes open vs. closed balance)

Sensory:
- Light touch, pinprick (spinothalamic tract)
- Vibration (128 Hz tuning fork), proprioception (posterior columns)
- Temperature

UPPER vs. LOWER MOTOR NEURON LESION

Upper Motor Neuron (UMN) — brain and spinal cord:
- Increased tone (spasticity)
- Hyperreflexia
- Extensor plantar (Babinski positive)
- No or mild atrophy
- Weakness in pattern (extensors in arm, flexors in leg)

Lower Motor Neuron (LMN) — anterior horn, nerve root, peripheral nerve:
- Decreased tone (flaccidity)
- Hyporeflexia or areflexia
- Flexor plantar
- Muscle wasting, fasciculations
- Weakness in distribution of nerve/root

NEUROIMAGING INDICATIONS
CT brain (non-contrast):
- Acute stroke (exclude hemorrhage before thrombolysis)
- Head trauma
- Emergency setting
- Contraindication to MRI

MRI brain:
- Superior soft tissue contrast
- Demyelinating disease (FLAIR/T2)
- Posterior fossa pathology
- Epilepsy imaging protocol
- Tumor assessment

CT angiography / MR angiography:
- Vascular disease, aneurysm, AVM, carotid stenosis

KEY NEUROLOGICAL SYNDROMES

Brown-Sequard syndrome (hemisection of spinal cord):
- Ipsilateral: UMN weakness, loss of proprioception and vibration
- Contralateral: pain and temperature loss (1-2 levels below)

Central cord syndrome (most common incomplete SCI):
- Greater upper limb than lower limb weakness
- Bladder dysfunction
- Variable sensory loss below lesion
- Hyperextension injury in elderly with cervical spondylosis

Cauda equina syndrome (compression below L1):
- Lower motor neuron signs in legs
- Saddle anesthesia (perineal numbness)
- Urinary/bowel dysfunction (retention, incontinence)
- SURGICAL EMERGENCY

Horner's syndrome (sympathetic chain disruption):
- Ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis, enophthalmos (pseudo-enophthalmos)
- Central: hypothalamus to T1 (Wallenberg, MS, tumor)
- Preganglionic: T1 root, superior cervical ganglion (Pancoast tumor, neck trauma)
- Postganglionic: cavernous sinus, ICA dissection, cluster headache

Internuclear ophthalmoplegia (MLF lesion):
- Ipsilateral adduction palsy on lateral gaze
- Contralateral nystagmus of abducting eye
- Bilateral: MS classic; unilateral: brainstem stroke

NEURO-OPHTHALMOLOGY
Papilledema (bilateral disc swelling):
- Raised ICP: brain tumor, IIH, hydrocephalus, venous sinus thrombosis
- Asymmetric: usually indicates local pathology

Visual field defects:
- Monocular blindness: ipsilateral optic nerve
- Bitemporal hemianopia: optic chiasm compression (pituitary tumor)
- Homonymous hemianopia: optic tract or radiation
- Quadrantanopia: partial radiation lesion (temporal = upper quadrant loss)
- Macular sparing: occipital lobe (dual blood supply)

Optic neuritis:
- Painful vision loss, central scotoma, reduced color vision
- Relative afferent pupillary defect (RAPD) on swinging flashlight test
- Most common cause: MS

CONSCIOUSNESS AND COMA
Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS):
- Eye opening: spontaneous (4), to voice (3), to pain (2), none (1)
- Verbal: oriented (5), confused (4), words (3), sounds (2), none (1)
- Motor: obeys commands (6), localizes (5), flexion/withdrawal (4), abnormal flexion (3), extension (2), none (1)
- Maximum 15, minimum 3

Brain death:
- No brainstem reflexes, apnea test
- Confirmatory tests: EEG (isoelectric), cerebral blood flow studies

Herniation syndromes:
- Uncal herniation: CN III palsy (dilated pupil), contralateral hemiplegia
- Central herniation: bilateral pupil changes, decerebrate posturing
- Tonsillar herniation: compression of foramen magnum, respiratory arrest

COMMON NEUROLOGICAL INVESTIGATIONS

Lumbar puncture (LP):
- Opening pressure, glucose, protein, cells, culture, cytology
- Oligoclonal bands (MS), xanthochromia (SAH after 12h)
- Contraindications: papilledema (image first), coagulopathy, local infection

Electroencephalography (EEG):
- Seizures, encephalopathy, brain death
- Epileptiform discharges, slowing, PLEDS

Nerve conduction studies (NCS/EMG):
- Characterize peripheral nerve and muscle disease
- Axonal vs. demyelinating
- Sensory vs. motor vs. mixed

Neuropsychological testing:
- Detailed cognitive assessment
- Dementia workup, pre-surgical assessment

LUMBAR SPINAL PATHOLOGY

Disc herniation:
- L4/5: L5 root: foot drop, numbness dorsum foot/1st toe
- L5/S1: S1 root: ankle reflex loss, numbness lateral foot/sole, plantarflexion weakness

Lumbar stenosis:
- Neurogenic claudication: leg pain/weakness with walking, relieved by sitting/bending forward
- Distinguished from vascular claudication by posture effect

Spondylolisthesis: forward slippage of vertebra
