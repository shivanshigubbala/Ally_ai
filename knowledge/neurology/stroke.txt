Stroke — Clinical Reference

DEFINITION AND OVERVIEW
A stroke occurs when blood supply to part of the brain is cut off, causing brain cells to die. There are two main types: ischemic stroke (87% of cases), caused by a blood clot blocking a vessel, and hemorrhagic stroke, caused by bleeding in or around the brain. A transient ischemic attack (TIA) is a temporary blockage that resolves within 24 hours but is a major warning sign.

SYMPTOMS — FAST ACRONYM
- Face drooping: One side of the face droops or is numb. Ask the person to smile.
- Arm weakness: One arm is weak or numb. Ask to raise both arms — does one drift down?
- Speech difficulty: Slurred speech, unable to speak, or strange speech.
- Time to call emergency services immediately.

Additional symptoms: sudden severe headache ("thunderclap"), vision loss in one or both eyes, sudden confusion, trouble walking, dizziness, loss of balance or coordination.

RISK FACTORS
- Hypertension (most important modifiable risk factor)
- Atrial fibrillation (cardioembolic stroke)
- Diabetes mellitus
- Hyperlipidemia
- Smoking
- Obesity and physical inactivity
- Prior stroke or TIA
- Carotid artery disease
- Sleep apnea

ISCHEMIC STROKE SUBTYPES
1. Cardioembolic: clot from heart (AF, valvular disease, cardiomyopathy)
2. Large artery atherosclerosis: carotid or vertebrobasilar stenosis
3. Small vessel (lacunar): hypertensive arteriolosclerosis, deep perforating arteries
4. Cryptogenic: no cause found despite workup

HEMORRHAGIC STROKE
- Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH): hypertensive, amyloid angiopathy, AVM, coagulopathy
- Subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH): ruptured aneurysm, sudden "worst headache of life"

DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP
- Urgent brain CT (non-contrast) to exclude hemorrhage
- CT angiography or MR angiography for vessel imaging
- MRI brain (DWI sequence most sensitive for acute ischemia)
- ECG, cardiac monitoring for AF
- Echocardiogram
- Carotid doppler ultrasound
- CBC, coagulation profile, lipid panel, glucose, HbA1c

ACUTE MANAGEMENT
- IV tPA (alteplase) within 4.5 hours of symptom onset for ischemic stroke (if no contraindications)
- Mechanical thrombectomy for large vessel occlusion within 6-24 hours
- Blood pressure management (permissive hypertension in ischemic stroke acutely; aggressive lowering in hemorrhagic)
- Aspirin 325mg within 24-48 hours (after excluding hemorrhage and if no tPA given)
- ICU monitoring for large strokes

SECONDARY PREVENTION
- Antiplatelet therapy (aspirin, clopidogrel) for non-cardioembolic stroke
- Anticoagulation (warfarin, NOACs) for cardioembolic stroke/AF
- Statin therapy for all ischemic stroke patients
- Antihypertensives (target BP <130/80)
- Carotid endarterectomy for >70% symptomatic stenosis
- Lifestyle modification: smoking cessation, diet, exercise

REHABILITATION
- Multidisciplinary approach: physiotherapy, occupational therapy, speech therapy
- Early mobilization improves outcomes
- Post-stroke depression is common — screen and treat
- Cognitive rehabilitation for post-stroke cognitive impairment
