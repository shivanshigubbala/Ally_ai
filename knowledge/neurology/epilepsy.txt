Epilepsy — Clinical Reference

DEFINITION
Epilepsy is a chronic neurological disorder characterized by recurrent (≥2) unprovoked seizures more than 24 hours apart, or one unprovoked seizure with a high (>60%) risk of recurrence, or diagnosis of an epilepsy syndrome. A seizure is a transient occurrence of signs/symptoms due to abnormal excessive or synchronous neuronal activity in the brain.

SEIZURE CLASSIFICATION (ILAE 2017)
Focal onset (originates in one hemisphere):
- Focal aware (formerly simple partial)
- Focal impaired awareness (formerly complex partial)
- Focal to bilateral tonic-clonic

Generalized onset (involves both hemispheres from start):
- Tonic-clonic (grand mal): loss of consciousness, tonic phase then rhythmic clonic jerks, post-ictal confusion
- Absence (petit mal): brief staring spells 5-30 seconds, no post-ictal state
- Myoclonic: brief sudden muscle jerks, often morning
- Tonic: sudden stiffening
- Atonic (drop attacks): sudden loss of muscle tone

Unknown onset:
- When onset cannot be determined

COMMON EPILEPSY SYNDROMES
Childhood syndromes:
- Childhood absence epilepsy (CAE): 4-10 years, frequent absences, remits in adolescence
- Juvenile myoclonic epilepsy (JME): adolescence onset, morning myoclonus + GTCS, lifelong condition
- Benign epilepsy with centrotemporal spikes (BECTS): focal facial/hand seizures, remits by 16

Adult syndromes:
- Temporal lobe epilepsy (TLE): most common adult focal epilepsy, aura (deja vu, epigastric rise), automatisms
- Frontal lobe epilepsy: nocturnal, brief, complex motor behavior

Progressive myoclonic epilepsies:
- Unverricht-Lundborg, Lafora disease, neuronal ceroid lipofuscinoses

CAUSES
Structural: cortical dysplasia, hippocampal sclerosis, tumors, stroke, trauma, AVM
Genetic: ion channel mutations, single-gene disorders
Infectious: neurocysticercosis (most common worldwide), HSV encephalitis, bacterial meningitis
Metabolic: hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, hypocalcemia, uremia
Immune: autoimmune encephalitis (anti-NMDAR, LGI1, CASPR2)
Unknown (idiopathic)

DIAGNOSTIC WORKUP
History:
- Detailed seizure description from witness
- Preceding aura, trigger factors
- Post-ictal state (confusion, Todd's paresis)
- Family history, birth history, developmental milestones
- History of febrile seizures, head trauma, CNS infections

Investigations:
- EEG (interictal and if possible ictal): key investigation
- MRI brain (protocol: coronal T1, FLAIR, DWI) — preferred over CT
- Blood tests: glucose, electrolytes, calcium, magnesium, CBC, LFTs, TFTs
- If relevant: metabolic screen, genetic testing, autoimmune antibodies, LP for CSF

DIFFERENTIAL DIAGNOSIS
- Syncope (vasovagal, cardiac) — most common mimic
- Psychogenic non-epileptic attacks (PNEA/PNES)
- Transient ischemic attacks
- Hemiplegic migraine
- Hypoglycemia
- Sleep disorders (parasomnia, REM behavior disorder)
- Hyperventilation, panic attacks

ANTI-SEIZURE MEDICATIONS (ASMs)
Broad spectrum (generalized + focal):
- Levetiracetam (Keppra): first-line, well tolerated, IV available
- Lamotrigine: effective, mood-stabilizing, slow titration required
- Valproate: highly effective for generalized, avoid in women of childbearing age (teratogenic), weight gain
- Topiramate: cognitive side effects
- Zonisamide

Focal seizures:
- Carbamazepine/Oxcarbazepine: first-line for focal, hyponatremia risk
- Lacosamide: good for focal, IV available
- Brivaracetam

Absence epilepsy:
- Ethosuximide (first-line for pure absence)
- Valproate (if GTCS also present)

TREATMENT PRINCIPLES
- Start with monotherapy
- Titrate gradually to effective dose
- If first drug fails at adequate dose, try alternative monotherapy before polytherapy
- ~70% of patients achieve seizure freedom with medication
- Drug-resistant epilepsy (DRE): failure of 2 adequate trials of appropriate ASMs

DRUG-RESISTANT EPILEPSY OPTIONS
- Surgical resection (best outcomes for temporal lobe epilepsy with hippocampal sclerosis)
- Vagus nerve stimulation (VNS)
- Responsive neurostimulation (RNS)
- Deep brain stimulation (DBS)
- Ketogenic diet (especially children)
- Laser interstitial thermal therapy (LITT)

STATUS EPILEPTICUS
- Defined as seizure lasting >5 minutes or two seizures without recovery in between
- Medical emergency with ~20% mortality if untreated
- Treatment protocol:
  1. Benzodiazepine (lorazepam IV, diazepam IV/PR, midazolam IM)
  2. Second-line: levetiracetam IV, valproate IV, or fosphenytoin
  3. Third-line (refractory): phenobarbital, propofol, midazolam infusion, ketamine

DRIVING AND LIFESTYLE
- Most countries require seizure-free period (typically 6-12 months) before driving
- Avoid heights, water activities alone, operating heavy machinery
- Seizure action plan for patient and caregivers
- Women of childbearing age: folic acid supplementation, medication teratogenicity counseling
- SUDEP (Sudden Unexpected Death in Epilepsy): risk highest with uncontrolled nocturnal GTCS
