General Physician — Common Conditions Clinical Reference

OVERVIEW
The general physician (GP) manages undifferentiated illness, chronic disease, preventive care, and coordinates specialist referrals. This knowledge base covers common presentations seen in general practice.

FEVER AND INFECTIOUS ILLNESS

Acute Febrile Illness:
- Temperature >38°C (100.4°F) is fever
- Common causes: viral URTI, influenza, UTI, pneumonia, gastroenteritis
- Red flags: temperature >39.5°C, rigors, rash, altered consciousness, neck stiffness, severe headache, hypotension

Influenza:
- Abrupt onset fever, myalgias, headache, cough, fatigue
- Treatment: supportive; antivirals (oseltamivir) within 48 hours for high-risk patients
- High-risk: elderly, immunocompromised, pregnancy, chronic disease
- Prevention: annual vaccination

COVID-19:
- Fever, cough, dyspnea, anosmia, myalgia, fatigue
- Spectrum: mild illness to critical respiratory failure
- High-risk groups: elderly, comorbidities (diabetes, CVD, obesity, immunocompromised)
- Treatment: antivirals (nirmatrelvir/ritonavir, molnupiravir for high-risk mild-moderate), supportive care
- Long COVID: ongoing symptoms >12 weeks post-infection

Urinary Tract Infection (UTI):
- Uncomplicated: dysuria, frequency, urgency; women
- Complicated: male, pregnancy, structural abnormality, recurrent, pyelonephritis
- Treatment: nitrofurantoin, trimethoprim (3 days for uncomplicated); cefalexin, amoxicillin-clavulanate
- Pyelonephritis: ciprofloxacin or co-amoxiclav 7-14 days; IV antibiotics if severe

Upper Respiratory Tract Infection (URTI):
- Predominantly viral; antibiotics NOT indicated
- Symptomatic: paracetamol, ibuprofen, decongestants, lozenges
- Sinusitis: usually viral; bacterial sinusitis if >10 days, fever, purulent discharge (amoxicillin)
- Strep throat: rapid test or culture; penicillin V if positive
- Antibiotic stewardship: avoid unnecessary prescriptions

Pneumonia:
- Community-acquired: fever, cough, purulent sputum, pleuritic chest pain
- CURB-65 scoring: confusion, urea, RR, BP, age >65 (score 0-1: home; 2: hospital; 3+: HDU)
- Common pathogens: Streptococcus pneumoniae, atypical organisms (Mycoplasma, Legionella, Chlamydia)
- Treatment: amoxicillin 500mg TDS 5 days for mild CAP; add clarithromycin for atypical

RESPIRATORY CONDITIONS

Asthma:
- Reversible airflow obstruction, bronchospasm, airway hyperresponsiveness
- Symptoms: wheeze, cough (often nocturnal), chest tightness, dyspnea
- Triggers: allergens, infections, exercise, cold air, NSAIDs, beta-blockers
- Investigations: spirometry (FEV1/FVC <0.7, >12% reversibility after bronchodilator)
- SABA (salbutamol) for acute relief
- ICS (inhaled corticosteroids): first-line preventer
- LABA added if poor control
- Severe/uncontrolled: monoclonal antibodies (omalizumab, mepolizumab, dupilumab)
- Acute severe: prednisolone 40mg, nebulized salbutamol, ipratropium
- Life-threatening: IV magnesium, HDU/ICU

COPD:
- Persistent, progressive airflow limitation; not fully reversible
- Risk factors: smoking (90%), occupational exposures, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency
- Symptoms: chronic cough, sputum, dyspnea, exercise limitation
- Spirometry: post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC <0.7
- GOLD staging (I-IV) based on FEV1
- Treatment: smoking cessation (most important), pulmonary rehabilitation
- SABA/SAMA for mild; LABA/LAMA for moderate; ICS added for frequent exacerbations
- Exacerbation: prednisolone 30-40mg for 5 days, antibiotics if purulent sputum
- Oxygen: long-term if PaO2 <7.3 kPa

CARDIOVASCULAR CONDITIONS

Hypertension:
- Target: <140/90 (most patients); <130/80 (high cardiovascular risk, diabetics)
- First-line: ACE inhibitor or ARB (particularly if diabetes, CKD, heart failure)
- Calcium channel blocker (CCB): good for all; especially African descent, elderly
- Thiazide diuretic: effective, cheap, add-on
- Beta-blocker: if heart failure, post-MI, rate control
- Lifestyle: salt restriction, weight loss, alcohol reduction, exercise, DASH diet
- Hypertensive urgency: DBP >120, end-organ damage — IV labetalol, hydralazine

Atrial Fibrillation:
- Most common sustained arrhythmia
- Risk: stroke (CHA2DS2-VASc score) — anticoagulate if score ≥2 men, ≥3 women
- Anticoagulants: DOACs preferred (apixaban, rivaroxaban, dabigatran, edoxaban); warfarin if valvular AF
- Rate control: beta-blockers, diltiazem, digoxin
- Rhythm control: cardioversion, antiarrhythmic drugs, ablation

Heart Failure:
- Reduced ejection fraction (HFrEF, EF <40%): ACE inhibitor/ARB/ARNI + beta-blocker + MRA + SGLT2 inhibitor (foundational four)
- Preserved EF (HFpEF): diuretics for congestion, risk factor management, SGLT2 inhibitors
- Symptoms: dyspnea, orthopnea, PND, ankle edema, fatigue
- Investigations: BNP, echocardiogram, CXR
- Diuretics: furosemide for fluid overload

DIABETES MELLITUS

Type 2 Diabetes:
- Lifestyle first: diet, exercise, weight loss
- First-line: Metformin (if eGFR >30, no contraindication)
- With established CVD/CKD: add SGLT2 inhibitor (empagliflozin) or GLP-1 agonist (semaglutide)
- HbA1c target: <48 mmol/mol (6.5%) general; <53 (7%) for elderly/complex
- Sulfonylureas (glipizide, gliclazide): hypoglycemia risk
- DPP4 inhibitors (sitagliptin): weight neutral, safe in renal disease
- Insulin: when oral agents fail or type 1
- Complications screening: annual eye check, annual foot exam, urine ACR, renal function, blood pressure
- Type 1: insulin required (MDI or pump); carb counting; CGMS

Hypoglycemia:
- Glucose <4 mmol/L with symptoms
- 15g fast-acting carbohydrate (glucose gel, juice)
- Severe/unconscious: glucagon IM or IV dextrose
- Review medication dosing, timing, food intake

GASTROINTESTINAL CONDITIONS

Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD):
- Heartburn, regurgitation, acid taste
- Lifestyle: weight loss, head of bed elevation, avoid triggers (fatty food, caffeine, alcohol, large meals, late eating)
- PPI (omeprazole, lansoprazole): first-line; take 30-60 min before meals
- H2 blockers (ranitidine, famotidine): less effective
- Alarm symptoms requiring endoscopy: dysphagia, weight loss, bleeding, anaemia, vomiting

Peptic Ulcer Disease:
- Gastric or duodenal ulcer
- Causes: H. pylori (80%), NSAIDs, stress
- Symptoms: epigastric pain, worse/better with food, nausea
- H. pylori testing: urea breath test, stool antigen, serology
- Triple therapy: PPI + amoxicillin + clarithromycin for 7-14 days
- NSAID ulcer: stop NSAID, add PPI

Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS):
- Chronic recurrent abdominal pain, bloating, altered bowel habit (no structural disease)
- Rome IV criteria: pain ≥1 day/week, associated with defecation or stool change
- Reassurance, dietary modification (low FODMAP diet), fiber
- Antispasmodics: mebeverine, hyoscine
- For diarrhea: loperamide, cholestyramine
- For constipation: osmotic laxatives
- Psychological therapies: CBT, hypnotherapy
- Low-dose TCA or SSRI for pain modulation

MUSCULOSKELETAL CONDITIONS

Back Pain:
- Mechanical (95%): muscle strain, disc disease, facet joints
- Non-specific low back pain: no imaging needed acutely (<6 weeks)
- Red flags (serious pathology): night pain, progressive neurological deficit, bowel/bladder dysfunction, fever, trauma, cancer history, age >50 new onset
- Treatment: reassurance, stay active, avoid bed rest, paracetamol ± NSAIDs, physiotherapy
- Chronic LBP: multimodal — exercise, CBT, manual therapy

Osteoarthritis:
- Degeneration of joint cartilage; hands, knees, hips most common
- Pain with use, stiffness after rest, crepitus, joint deformity
- X-ray: joint space narrowing, osteophytes, subchondral sclerosis
- Treatment: weight loss, exercise (water exercise, physiotherapy), paracetamol, topical NSAIDs, oral NSAIDs short-term, intra-articular steroid injections, joint replacement for severe

Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA):
- Chronic inflammatory arthritis; synovial inflammation
- Symmetrical small joint arthritis (MCP, PIP), morning stiffness >1 hour
- Extra-articular: rheumatoid nodules, pulmonary fibrosis, vasculitis, scleritis
- RF and anti-CCP antibodies (specific)
- Treatment: DMARDs early (methotrexate first-line), hydroxychloroquine, sulfasalazine
- Biological DMARDs: TNF inhibitors (adalimumab, etanercept), rituximab, tocilizumab
- Low-dose prednisolone as bridge therapy

MENTAL HEALTH (PRIMARY CARE)

Depression:
- Persistent low mood, anhedonia, fatigue, sleep disturbance, poor concentration, guilt, suicidal ideation
- PHQ-9 for screening and severity
- Mild: watchful waiting, lifestyle, self-help, CBT
- Moderate-severe: SSRIs (sertraline, fluoxetine first-line), CBT
- Refer if psychotic features, severe risk, treatment-resistant

Anxiety Disorders:
- GAD: excessive worry, physical symptoms, difficult to control
- Panic disorder: discrete panic attacks
- Social anxiety, specific phobias, OCD, PTSD
- Screening: GAD-7
- Treatment: CBT, SSRIs, SNRIs; avoid benzodiazepines long-term

PREVENTIVE CARE

Cancer Screening:
- Breast: mammography 2-yearly from 50-74 years
- Cervical: HPV-based testing or Pap smear (frequency varies by country)
- Colorectal: FIT stool test from 50 years, colonoscopy if positive
- Prostate: PSA testing discussion with patient (harms vs. benefits)
- Lung: low-dose CT for heavy smokers 50-80 years

Immunizations (adults):
- Annual influenza
- COVID-19 vaccination/boosters
- Pneumococcal (PCV15/PCV20 for ≥65)
- Shingles (Shingrix) for ≥50
- Tdap (tetanus, diphtheria, pertussis)
- Hepatitis B (if not previously vaccinated)

Cardiovascular Risk Assessment:
- 10-year CVD risk tools: Framingham, QRISK3, ACC/AHA pooled cohort equation
- Statins for primary prevention if 10-year risk ≥10% (discuss with patient)
- Aspirin: no longer routine for primary prevention (bleeding risk)
