Cardiac Diagnostic Tests — Overview

This reference summarizes the tests the cardiology agent is permitted to
recommend (matching backend/specialties/cardiology/guidelines.json), what
each test is for, and roughly when it is appropriate — so recommendations
made during a consultation are explainable to the patient.

ECG (Electrocardiogram): a quick, noninvasive recording of the heart's
electrical activity. First-line for chest pain, palpitations, syncope, or
any suspected arrhythmia or ischemia. Painless, takes only a few minutes.

Troponin (blood test): measures a protein released into the blood when
heart muscle is injured. The key test for diagnosing or ruling out a heart
attack in someone with chest pain concerning for acute coronary syndrome.
Often repeated after a few hours since levels can take time to rise.

Echocardiogram (cardiac ultrasound): a noninvasive ultrasound of the heart
that shows chamber size, wall motion, pumping strength (ejection
fraction), and valve function. Useful for shortness of breath, suspected
heart failure, murmurs, and structural concerns. Painless, no radiation.

Holter Monitor: a portable ECG worn continuously for 24-48 hours (or
longer with newer patch monitors) to capture intermittent arrhythmias that
a single resting ECG would miss. Appropriate for palpitations or suspected
arrhythmia that isn't caught on a resting ECG.

Lipid Profile (blood test): measures LDL, HDL, triglycerides, and total
cholesterol. Used for cardiovascular risk assessment, particularly
relevant for patients with risk factors, family history, or existing
cardiovascular disease, even if not the primary complaint.

Blood Pressure Monitoring (home or ambulatory): repeated blood pressure
readings over time, used to confirm a hypertension diagnosis, assess
control of known hypertension, and rule out "white coat" effects from a
single clinic reading.

Chest X-Ray: a plain radiograph of the chest, useful for assessing heart
size (an enlarged cardiac silhouette can suggest heart failure or
cardiomyopathy) and for looking at the lungs to rule out fluid, pneumonia,
or other pulmonary causes of breathlessness.

BNP / NT-proBNP (blood test): a hormone released by the heart under
strain. Elevated levels support a diagnosis of heart failure and help
gauge its severity; useful when shortness of breath or leg swelling raises
concern for heart failure.

Stress Test (exercise or pharmacologic): evaluates how the heart responds
to increased demand, used to investigate suspected coronary artery disease
when someone has exertional chest pain or risk factors but the resting
ECG and other basic tests are inconclusive. Not typically a first-line
test in an acute triage conversation — usually appropriate after initial
evaluation has stabilized the patient and ruled out an acute event.

General principle for recommending tests: match the test to the symptom
pattern and risk level, explain briefly why each test is being suggested,
and avoid recommending tests when the symptom picture is mild, self-limited,
and without red flags or significant risk factors — over-testing can cause
unnecessary anxiety and cost without clinical benefit.
